UX Case Study: Zomato

CodeAlpha Internship — Month 1 · Task 3 (UI/UX Design) · Prepared by: Intern

1. Introduction

Zomato is a leading food-delivery and restaurant-discovery platform. This case study analyzes its user journey, highlights strengths and weaknesses, and proposes three evidence-based improvements with mockups.

2. User Journey Analysis

  1. Discovery: User opens app → sees banners, collections, search bar.
  2. Search & Filter: Types restaurant/dish → applies cuisine/price/rating filters.
  3. Selection: Views restaurant page → menu, ratings, reviews, photos.
  4. Cart: Adds items → customizes (size, toppings) → reviews cart.
  5. Checkout: Selects address → payment → applies coupon → places order.
  6. Tracking: Live order tracking → delivery → rating/review.

3. Strengths

Fast search & rich filters: Quick restaurant/dish search with cuisine, price, rating filters.
Live order tracking: Real-time map tracking builds trust and reduces anxiety.
Multiple payment options: Cards, UPI, wallets, COD — broad accessibility.
Reviews & photos: User-generated content aids decision-making.
Personalized collections: "Best of" lists surface relevant choices.

4. Weaknesses

Cluttered home screen: Too many banners, collections and pop-ups overwhelm first-time users.
Aggressive upselling: Pop-ups for Zomato Gold/Pro interrupt the ordering flow.
Inconsistent filters: Filter placement differs between search and restaurant pages.
Checkout friction: Address selection and coupon application require multiple taps.
Accessibility gaps: Low contrast on some labels; small tap targets on chips.
Dark patterns: Pre-selected "donate a meal" and auto-applied tips.

5. Proposed Improvements

Improvement 1 — Simplified Home & Smart Search

Discovery Reduce cognitive load by decluttering the home screen and making search the primary action.

  • Single prominent search bar at top; categories as horizontal scrollable chips below.
  • Limit to 3 featured collections; move rest to "Explore" tab.
  • Remove auto pop-ups; show offers as dismissible inline cards.

Improvement 2 — Streamlined Checkout

Conversion Cut checkout steps from 5 taps to 2 by remembering preferences.

  • Auto-select last-used address and payment method.
  • One-tap coupon application with best-offer auto-suggest.
  • Remove pre-selected donations/tips; make them opt-in with clear labels.

Improvement 3 — Personalized & Inclusive Discovery

Engagement Help users find food that fits their needs and diet.

  • Dietary filters (veg/vegan/gluten-free) persisted across sessions.
  • "Reorder" shortcut on home for last 3 orders.
  • Improved contrast and larger tap targets (min 44px) for accessibility.

6. Conclusion

The proposed changes target the three highest-friction areas: discovery clutter, checkout length, and personalization/accessibility. Together they should reduce time-to-order, increase conversion, and improve inclusivity while preserving Zomato's strengths in tracking and payments.
